Newborn Screening (NBS)

Newborn screening is a simple blood test done 24–72 hours after birth to detect conditions that can cause serious illness or disability if not treated early. In the Philippines, NBS is available in hospitals, lying-in clinics, and rural health units.

  • Done by pricking the baby's heel and collecting a few drops of blood on filter paper.
  • Screens for congenital hypothyroidism, galactosemia, phenylketonuria, and more.
  • Early detection and treatment can prevent mental retardation and death.
  • Ask your doctor or midwife about the Expanded Newborn Screening program.

Wellness & Preventive Care

Small daily habits make a big difference in long-term health.

Eat Right

Follow Go, Grow, Glow foods. Limit salty, fatty, and sugary meals.

Move Daily

Aim for 30 minutes of activity — walking, jogging, or household chores count.

Sleep Well

Get 7–9 hours of sleep. Keep a consistent bedtime schedule.

Get Checked

Visit your barangay health center or doctor for regular check-ups.

Mental Health

Rest, connect with loved ones, and seek help when you feel overwhelmed.

Vaccinations

Keep immunizations up to date — for children and adults alike.
